# Handwriting and draw-and-hold decisions

Research date: 2026-10-05. This is the rationale for 0.7.2, not a claim of parity with a tablet note-taking app.

## Evidence and choice

Apple documents that Notes uses an on-device model to refine handwriting while retaining personal style. Its PencilKit recognition APIs operate on native PKDrawing/PKStroke data and support recognition/search; that is different from making a sampled line less wobbly. An Obsidian desktop plugin does not acquire those features by rendering an SVG. This release offers **Smooth ink**, not Apple-style handwriting rewriting, OCR, spelling correction or text recognition. [Apple handwriting refinement](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/121259), [PencilKit recognition](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pencilkit/recognizing-handwriting-and-converting-to-text).

Perfect Freehand exposes both adjusted centerline samples (`getStrokePoints`, including streamlining) and pressure-dependent outline generation. Excalidraw lists it as a dependency. We use pinned version 1.2.3, MIT licensed, for moderate centerline stabilization after arc-length resampling. A small corner-cutting pass removes sample jitter. First and last points are retained. The resulting centerline is used for both live preview and standard PDF Ink output, avoiding two different render geometries. The toggle applies to new pen strokes only and defaults on. There is no simulated-pressure appearance or untested hardware-pressure claim. [Perfect Freehand API/source](https://github.com/steveruizok/perfect-freehand), [Excalidraw dependency manifest](https://github.com/excalidraw/excalidraw/blob/master/packages/excalidraw/package.json).

Goodnotes describes draw-and-hold as drawing a shape, then keeping the stylus down at the end until it snaps. That interaction is useful because recognition is explicitly requested by a pause, rather than applied to every handwritten character. Here a hold requires 650ms within 2.5 display pixels; normal movement restarts the interval. A visible label identifies the accepted shape. In 0.7.2, movement after snapping transforms the original fitted shape instead of resuming freehand. Lines track the held endpoint, arrows use a rotation/uniform-scale transform, circles change radius around their center, and other closed shapes stretch around the opposite bounds corner. Rectangle axes retain the drawn rotation. Page-edge interpolation bounds the complete transform. Each sample starts from the original fit, avoiding accumulated drift. Near-circular ellipse fits (principal radius ratio up to 1.25) receive equal radii and a distinct Circle label. Lifting commits one ordinary ink action; Escape cancels it. [Goodnotes draw-and-hold](https://www.goodnotes.com/blog/shape-recognition).

The $1 recognizer is a lightweight geometric template matcher for single strokes; its related $N and $P approaches address multiple strokes. A nearest-template answer still needs rejection thresholds and representative input data before it can safely replace handwriting. For the narrow diagram set here, geometric fitting makes rejection and PDF geometry explicit: endpoint/path error for lines, simplified corners and right-angle checks for rectangles, triangle area and edge fit, principal-axis ellipse fitting with radial and revolution tests, and shaft/wing/retrace checks for arrows. Arc-length resampling avoids letting a long dwell dominate a fit. Uncertain input stays freehand. This is our implementation decision, not a reported accuracy result from the paper. [University of Washington recognizer research](https://depts.washington.edu/acelab/proj/dollar/index.html).

## Shipped scope

- Highlighter: hold to straighten between its actual endpoints, preserving diagonal strokes. Enabled by default; Shift still constrains horizontal drawing.
- Pen: optional **Draw & hold shapes**, off by default so ordinary handwriting pauses are unaffected. Recognizes a single-stroke line, rectangle/square, triangle, ellipse/circle or arrow. An arrow is drawn along its shaft, to one wing, back to the tip and out to the opposite wing.
- Recognition preserves rotation and approximate dimensions. Fits outside the page are rejected. No endpoint joins between separate marks; no snapping multiple strokes together.
- Cube recognition is deferred. A cube usually contains several strokes, shared vertices and perspective edges. It needs grouping, topology constraints and a representative labeled corpus, plus reversible group edits. Treating a cube as the nearest single-stroke template would be unreliable and would add false positives to normal handwriting.

## 0.7.1 correction from real drawing feedback

The original regression inputs were too close to perfect geometry. Real worksheet strokes reproduced rejection of rounded rectangles, uneven circles and a rounded triangle. The recognizer now finds a nearly closed loop within the stroke, allowing a short lead-in or finishing hook (at most 20% of sampled travel discarded), before fitting. Polygon fitting tolerates rounded corners and moderate skew; a coarser triangle candidate still has to fit the original loop. Ellipse fitting permits moderate radial variation while preserving revolution/retrace rejection. Uncertain input still remains freehand.

Five position-normalized drawing samples from the anonymous development worksheet now run in both directions at three sizes and four rotations (120 cases). The original open-loop/spiral/scribble rejection checks remain. This expands the evidence beyond ideal fixtures; it still is not recognition-accuracy validation on a representative handwriting corpus. A deliberately held handwritten loop can be interpreted as a circle when assistance is enabled.

## Verification and remaining work

Automated checks cover rotated/reversed shapes, unequal sampling, jitter, endpoint fidelity, tap behavior, hold timing, and rejection of open loops, letters, spirals, retracing and ambiguous scribbles. Saved assisted strokes are independently read back as editable PDF Ink with matching points. These are regression fixtures, not measured recognition accuracy on a handwriting corpus.

Before expanding recognition: collect user-approved anonymous mouse and pen examples at different zoom levels; label intended shapes and ordinary handwriting; measure false positives, rejection rate and latency; test sustained writing and physical stylus interruption. Add cube/multistroke recognition only with that evidence and appropriate group editing. Pressure and handwriting-model integration remain separate projects.
